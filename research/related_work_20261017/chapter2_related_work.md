# 2 Related Work

## 2.1 Social Bias Evaluation and Benchmark Interpretation

BBQ evaluates social bias through paired ambiguous and disambiguated contexts: models should avoid unsupported group judgments when evidence is missing and follow the supplied evidence when it resolves the question \citep{bbq2022}. Its original design therefore already distinguishes these conditions. Nevertheless, improved aggregate performance need not identify the mechanism responsible for the improvement. Work on prompt-based debiasing documents evasive responses and limitations of interpreting bias metrics without checking contextual correctness \citep{rethinking2025}. More recently, single-example interventions have produced substantial BBQ gains by eliciting missing-evidence reasoning, questioning whether success on this benchmark establishes broader fairness \citep{oneexample2026}. Our study builds on these concerns by examining the incremental contribution of activation intervention once an evidence detector is available.

Other benchmarks broaden the evaluation target. CEB evaluates fairness across multiple task settings, while OpenBBQ studies open-ended formulations of bias evaluation \citep{ceb2025,openbbq2024}. These motivate testing beyond a fixed multiple-choice interface, although an open-ended reformulation does not by itself establish transfer to unrestricted generation. SynthBias and its uncertainty-aware evaluation framework further highlight information lost by discrete answer metrics \citep{synthbias2025}. We distinguish the original evaluation protocols from our task adaptations and separately measure performance on sufficient and insufficient contexts. In particular, aggregate target-task improvement can coexist with excessive abstention on answerable questions.

## 2.2 Activation Steering and Neuron-Level Debiasing

Representation engineering encompasses both reading and controlling internal representations \citep{repe2023}. Contrastive activation addition estimates behavioral directions from contrasting examples and adds them during inference \citep{caa2024}. Related work identifies a direction that mediates harmful-request refusal in studied chat models \citep{refusal2024}. Such findings motivate interventions in internal states, but harmful-request refusal and abstention due to missing evidence are different behaviors. Because our evidence classifier itself reads hidden representations, our comparison concerns activation steering rather than representation engineering as a whole.

Conditional methods also precede our work. CAST conditions steering on detected input properties \citep{cast2025}. FairSteer combines bias detection with dynamic activation steering for inference-time debiasing \citep{fairsteer2025}. Its bias detector and our evidence-sufficiency detector have different supervision targets: predicting a biased response is not equivalent to predicting insufficient context. We therefore distinguish comparisons between complete methods from comparisons that hold a detector fixed and change only its downstream action. Our small-model adaptations also do not constitute a refutation of FairSteer's results under its original configurations.

Neuron-level approaches offer alternative control mechanisms. CRISPR identifies and suppresses bias-associated neurons, whereas KnowBias enhances neurons associated with knowledge about bias \citep{crispr2024,knowbias2026}. Bi-directional Bias Attribution uses attribution to guide internal debiasing interventions \citep{bba2026}. Adaptive Triggering instead decides when to introduce textual bias correction during reasoning \citep{adaptive2026}. These methods differ in supervision, intervention location, and inference cost; they should not be treated as interchangeable implementations. Our controlled comparison asks whether the tested internal interventions improve on an explicit decision rule using the same evidence signal.

## 2.3 Evidence Sufficiency and Selective Answering

Answerability detection is an established problem. SQuAD 2.0 includes unanswerable questions, and selective question answering studies when a system should abstain, including under distribution shift \citep{squad2018,selectiveqa2020}. Work on sufficient context distinguishes whether the supplied information supports an answer from whether a particular model answers correctly, and combines sufficiency information with confidence for selective generation \citep{sufficient2025}. Consequently, neither evidence-aware abstention nor combining evidence and confidence constitutes a new contribution by itself.

Internal representations can also support predictions about truth or correctness \citep{internal2023,know2022}. Such readouts are relevant precedents, but contextual sufficiency, model confidence, and answer correctness are distinct targets. Furthermore, a successful probe does not establish that the original model causally uses the decoded information. Probe-control research motivates checks against learning or exploiting unintended signals \citep{probecontrols2019}; our surface-feature and length controls address related concerns without reproducing those papers' exact control-task protocols.

YOPO is particularly close in combining a sufficiency readout with activation steering. It studies interference between reading and writing a frozen model's residual stream and introduces a reconstruction mechanism to preserve the readout \citep{yopo2026}. Its reported benefits from combining the two operations caution against a general conclusion that steering adds no value. Our focus is narrower: on BBQ, we compare different actions under a shared evidence gate and evaluate their answerable-question costs and transfer limitations.

There is also a metric distinction. In conventional selective prediction, abstained examples are excluded when computing selective accuracy, and coverage is reported separately. On BBQ, selecting the explicit unknown option is a scored answer. We therefore retain all examples when reporting task accuracy and distinguish unknown-selection frequency from selective coverage. A threshold selected to limit false abstention on source development data is an empirical operating point, not a guarantee under distribution shift.

## 2.4 Strong Baselines and Detection versus Control

AxBench separates concept detection from steering and finds strong performance from simple baselines relative to the evaluated sparse-autoencoder methods \citep{axbench2025}. Subsequent work shows that selecting features for their effects on outputs can substantially improve SAE steering \citep{saefeatures2025}. Together, these results motivate simple controls while emphasizing that method rankings depend on feature selection and evaluation conditions. Since we do not evaluate SAEs, our findings do not establish a ranking for that method family.

Our contribution is a controlled empirical comparison of detection and action in BBQ-style debiasing. Holding an evidence gate fixed, we compare direct unknown selection with output-score adjustment and hidden-state steering, alongside surface-feature controls. We assess ambiguous-question corrections, sufficient-question errors, and transfer under source-selected operating points. This design tests what the evaluated activation intervention contributes beyond the detector and a task-aligned response rule. It does not establish a universal bias direction, a complete causal account of bias, or broad fairness from benchmark accuracy alone.
